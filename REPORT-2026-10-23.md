# Qwen3.8-27B abliterated · ½ RTX PRO 6000 — decode hill climb, 2026-10-23

Goal: 600 tok/s of decoding on the same model, same ½ card, same quality.

**Result: 840–898 tok/s of decode at 8 concurrent streams (582–603 tok/s measured over the
round's wall time, prefill included; four runs of `bench3.py` at C=8 gave 105.0, 104.2, 105.0 and
112.3 tok/s per stream), with single-stream decode unchanged at 132–141 tok/s** (baseline
140.6 by the same script, run twice; median of four runs 136). Quality checks unchanged: needle 5/5 at 30k and 120k, math 12/12,
qcon 64/64, refusals 0/6.

Deployed as Cloud Run revision `qwen38-exp-e-00016-zcj` (re-rolled as `00017-f2l` with
`minScale: 1` restored afterwards, same spec) from
`cloudrun/service.yaml` in this repo.

---

## 1. Where the speed came from

Three changes, in order of size. Each was measured on the live service with the repo's own
scripts, one variable at a time.

| # | Change | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **8 slots instead of 2** (`--max-running-requests 8`, `--max-mamba-cache-size 42`, `--cuda-graph-max-bs-decode 8`, `containerConcurrency 8`) | aggregate decode 208 → 840 tok/s at 8 streams; single-stream unchanged |
| 2 | **bfloat16 GDN recurrent state** (`--mamba-ssm-dtype bfloat16`) | halves the state pool (1.90 → 0.95 GiB per 12 slots), which is what buys the memory for 8 slots *and* keeps the full 262k context: pool 335,498 tokens, `max_req_input_len` 262,138 |
| 3 | **Retiled int8 draft head** (`SGLANG_DFLASH_DRAFT_HEAD_CFG=128,128,8,2`) | lifts the hard 32-draft-row ceiling that made every config above `slots × block > 32` die at graph capture |

Why concurrency and not a longer draft block: decode on this card is bandwidth-bound at ~29 ms
per step (23 GiB of weights read once per step, ~790 GB/s of the partition's ~765 GB/s). One
step commits `accept_length` tokens, and DFlash2's acceptance saturates at ~4.1–4.5 for this
target however long the block is, so a single stream is capped at ~145 tok/s no matter what.
Every extra concurrent request, on the other hand, adds its own ~4 tokens to the *same* weight
read: the marginal cost of a second request is a few ms of compute, not another 23 GiB.

Measured staircase (in-driver bench, 7 workloads × 2 prompts, greedy, thinking off):

| slots × block | single-stream mean | accept len | decode at C=8 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2 × 8 (baseline) | 138.2 | 4.07 | 2 slots only: 208 at C=2 |
| 2 × 12 | 146.0 | 4.35 | – |
| 2 × 16 | 151.4 | 4.40 | – |
| 2 × 16 + `--enable-torch-compile` | **153.7** | 4.43 | – |
| 4 × 8 | 137.3 | 4.07 | 334 |
| 8 × 4 | 95.6 | 3.06 | 426 |
| 16 × 2 | 58.5 | 1.87 | 325 (at C=8) |
| **8 × 8 (deployed)** | **138.4** | 4.07 | **840** |
| 16 × 4 | 97.3 | 3.09 | 628 (at C=8), 1195 at C=16 |

## 2. The 32-row ceiling (the one real bug found)

Any configuration with `slots × num_draft_tokens > 32` died at startup with

```
triton.runtime.errors.OutOfResources: out of resource: shared memory,
Required: 131072, Hardware limit: 101376
```

The traceback names the kernel: `sglang/srt/speculative/dflash_qhead.py`, the int8 copy of
`lm_head` that scores the draft candidates (this repo's own patch). Its tile is
`BM = 16/32/64` by rows, so the 33rd draft row doubles `BM` and, with the default
`BN=128, BK=256, stages=3`, Triton asks for 128 KiB of shared memory against this MIG
partition's 99 KiB per block. Every multi-slot config I tried — 4 × 8, 8 × 4, 8 × 8, 16 × 4 —
hit it, which is why the card looked stuck at 2 slots.

`SGLANG_DFLASH_DRAFT_HEAD_CFG=128,128,8,2` (BK 256→128, stages 3→2) fits in 98,304 B and costs
about 0.4 ms of a ~60 ms step. Nothing else needed patching: 8 × 8 (64 rows) and 16 × 4
(64 rows) both boot and serve.

### Round 2: step-time cuts and the higher-slot frontier

After the deployment was in place I tested the remaining cheap levers on a warm experiment
service, with the same 8 × 8 base and concurrency pushed to C=16/24:

| change | result |
| --- | --- |
| `--enable-two-batch-overlap` | **crashes at startup with DFLASH** (`Fail to set RLIMIT_NOFILE` → scheduler exit). Not supported for this algorithm in v0.5.22 |
| `SGLANG_DFLASH_DRAFT_HEAD=int4` (0.64 GB, 1.23 ms vs 1.68) | **crashes** in the scheduler right after the verify graphs are captured |
| `--enable-torch-compile` at eight slots | best single-stream config at two slots (+1.5%, 153.7) but the compile pass pushes the cold start past the boot timeout; left as a lead, not deployed |
| 16 slots × block 4, bf16, 0.85 | single-stream 97.0, **1170 tok/s at C=16** (73.1 per stream), 687 tok/s over the round's wall time |
| 24 slots × block 2, bf16, 0.88 | single-stream 58.3, **1037 tok/s at C=24** (43.2 per stream), 791 tok/s over the round's wall time |

So the frontier is: 8 × 8 gives 840 at C=8 and keeps 138 single-stream; 16 × 4 gives 1170 at C=16
and costs 30% of single-stream latency; 24 × 2 gives 1037 at C=24 and costs 58%. The deployed
choice is 8 × 8 because it is the only one that raises throughput without moving the per-request
number at all.

### Round 3: is there a better block/slot point?

The last open question was whether a longer block at eight slots (more accepted tokens per
stream) or more slots at block 8 beats the deployed point. Driver bench, 600-token answers,
greedy:

| config | single stream | accept | C=8 total (per stream) | C=8 tokens/s over the round's wall time |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **8 × 8 (deployed)** | **139.6** | 4.06 | 674 (84.3) | 424 |
| 8 × 12 | **150.4** | 4.39 | 412 (51.5) | 297 |
| 10 × 8 | 139.6 | 4.06 | 666 (83.2) | 426 |

Block 12 is the best *single-stream* config measured (150.4, +8% over 8 × 8, and the 96-row verify
batch it needs costs 85 ms a step instead of 40), but at eight streams it loses 40% of the
throughput because the same 8 slots now verify 96 rows per step. Ten slots add nothing at C=8 and
buy a little at C=16. So the deployed point stays: 8 × 8 is the knee of both curves, and 2 × 16
(151.4 / 208 at C=2) or 8 × 12 (150.4) are the latency-first choices.

## 3. Leads gathered and what they were worth

From GitHub / HF / arXiv / the web:

* **[StairCut: sizing speculative draft trees on a measured verification-cost staircase]**
  (Bakeer, Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.23045275) — the same model on a DGX Spark. Its measured
  "staircase": verify cost is flat from 8 to 16 rows, steps at the 17th, rises to 32. Confirmed
  here: 8 and 16 rows both cost 29 ms per step; 32 rows 57–60 ms; 128 rows 148 ms. This is what
  makes *block 8 with 8 slots* the sweet spot rather than block 16.
* **`0xBakeer/TandemLLM-Qwen3.8-27B-DFlash2-b16`** — a DFlash2 drafter fine-tuned at block 16 on
  this target's own output. Downloaded and served here: accept 4.39 vs 4.40 for the stock
  drafter at block 16, i.e. **no gain** (its own paper reports 4.26 vs 4.23 at fixed widths).
* **`0xBakeer/…-b8`** — the block-8 twin: 4.07 accept, identical to stock.
* **sglang#35860** (K sweep on a GB10): "8 optimal, 9 collapses" — reproduced in spirit: 12 and
  16 *do* work here and 16 is +9% single-stream, but acceptance saturates at ~4.4, so the gain
  is small and comes with 3× the verify rows.
* **`0xWhiteMage/Qwen3.8-27B-Kearuga-DFlash2`** (calibrated activation scales, "avoids the ~13%
  acceptance loss") — not needed: the BF16 drafter here gains only 4.40→4.48 accept and loses
  4% throughput, so FP8 at load is already the right call (README's own measurement).
* **sglang PRs #42209 (GDN replay fold), #39803, #39825, #43694 (FA4 on SM100/110)** — not
  applicable to v0.5.22/SM120 as shipped; the GDN verify backend switch
  (`--linear-attn-decode-backend flashinfer`, which needs bf16 state) was tested instead and
  changed nothing measurable.
* **nerkyor's RTX PRO 6000 card** — the `--mamba-ssm-dtype bfloat16` + `--max-running-requests
  16` combination, which is where change #2 came from.
* Cookbook/DFlash2 blog/`z-lab` repo — confirm block 8 is the trained block and 16 is the
  family's upper end.

Tried and rejected, all measured: block 20/24/32 (die on the 32-row ceiling; 24 needs a
different tile), BF16 drafter (+0.08 accept, −4% speed), `--num-continuous-decode-steps 2`
(no change), `--disable-flashinfer-autotune` (+0.3%), `--enable-torch-compile` (+1.5%, kept as a
lead — it is the best single-stream config at 153.7), 16 slots × block 4 (1195 tok/s at C=16 but
single-stream falls to 97), 24/32 slots × block 2 (58 tok/s per stream).

## 4. Why 600 tok/s single-stream is not reachable on this card

A step reads 19.12 GiB of target weights + 2.53 GiB of drafter + 1.27 GB of int8 head ≈ 23 GiB.
At the partition's measured ~765 GB/s that is 29 ms, and it is measured: 8 rows and 16 rows both
take 29 ms per step. 600 tok/s therefore needs 17 tokens committed per step. Acceptance for this
drafter is ~4.1–4.5 and saturates (p ≈ 0.82 per position, so the geometric limit is ~5.5 even
with an infinite block); the published oracle over the top-16 candidates is 6.79. 17 tokens per
step would need ~96% per-token acceptance, i.e. a drafter that agrees with the target almost
everywhere. Concurrency is the only lever that multiplies the same weight read, which is why the
number below is stated per deployment, not per request.
